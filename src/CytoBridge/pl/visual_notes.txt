(2025).Data Structure (Generic):Static Snapshots: A set of discrete experimental observations at multiple time points (e.g., $T_1, T_2, ..., T_n$).Dynamic Trajectories: Multiple continuous paths generated by a model, flowing through these time points.Visual Design Instructions (The "Vinyard" Style):The "Manifold Background" (Discrete Points):Layering: Treat experimental data as a secondary background layer.Styling: Use very small marker sizes and high transparency (alpha ≈ 0.1).Coloring: Use a discrete, professional palette (e.g., 'Set2' or 'tab10') to distinguish different time points. This creates a "shadow" of the biological state space.The "Continuous Flow" (Model Trajectories):Layering: These are the primary focus. They should sit on top of the background.Styling: Thin, smooth lines with a subtle alpha (≈ 0.6) to allow overlapping paths to create a density effect.Time-Gradient Mapping: Crucial. Apply a continuous colormap (like 'viridis', 'magma', or 'GnBu') along the length of each trajectory. The color should transition based on the normalized time value (0 to 1), regardless of the specific days/hours.Endpoint Markers: Add a small, solid, opaque dot at the final point of each trajectory to indicate the "fate" or destination.Refined Composition (Nature-Style Aesthetics):Axis Style: Use sns.despine() to remove top and right borders. Remove grid lines for a clean, "airy" look.Typography: All labels and legends must use a clean sans-serif font (Arial or Helvetica).Colorbar: Include a slim, elegant colorbar labeled "Normalized Time" or "Progression" instead of a crowded legend.Aspect Ratio: Maintain a 1:1 aspect ratio so the geometric relationships in the embedding (e.g., UMAP/PCA) are not distorted.Goal: The resulting plot should elegantly bridge the gap between static experimental "snapshots" and the continuous "motion" of the model, using transparency and color gradients to convey complex temporal dynamics.
